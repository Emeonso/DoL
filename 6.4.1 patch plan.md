# Degrees of Lewdity 6.4.1 patch plan

## Multi-faced protagonist goals and route framework

This is an exploratory design plan, not a locked implementation specification. It captures the baseline direction for a future patch and intentionally leaves route structures, thresholds, and individual mechanics open for later refinement.

## Summary

Give the protagonist a clear long-term project from the opening, while allowing the game to develop several competing interpretations of what “success” means.

The erotic content remains the game’s central loop. The background systems exist to give those encounters continuity, consequences, and direction. School, work, reputation, relationships, pregnancy, and independence should feel like parts of the protagonist’s attempt to build or lose a future, rather than time-filling activities between sex scenes.

The framework should remain exploratory. The structures and route ideas below are candidate directions, not final selections.

## Existing life-direction systems

The relevant background layer consists of systems that answer what the protagonist is doing with their life. Most other mechanics support or complicate these tracks rather than constituting life directions of their own.

- **School and education:** gives the protagonist a routine, social environment, measure of progress, and possible future beyond the town’s immediate sexual economy.
- **Work and economic role:** covers ordinary jobs, businesses, prostitution, income, and dependence on employers or customers. Money is the resource produced by this track, not a separate life direction.
- **Reputation and social position:** describes what the town believes the protagonist is and determines which social roles, opportunities, dangers, and communities become available.
- **Relationships and dependence:** covers Robin, named NPCs, recurring connections, protection, affection, ownership, obligations, and shared routes. It determines who becomes part of the protagonist’s future.
- **Pregnancy and family:** creates an immediate change to the protagonist’s body, responsibilities, relationships, and ability or desire to leave. It can lead towards parenthood, dependence, protection, exploitation, or a new reason to escape.
- **Housing and independence:** describes whether the protagonist remains under Bailey’s control, becomes dependent on someone else, obtains a home, or establishes an independent life. The native game treats this more as recurring pressure than a complete progression track, but it is the natural frame for the proposed large goal.

Skills, money, clothing, transport, time, health, inventory, and weather fold into these tracks as enabling or obstructing mechanics. Trauma, control, desire, sexual history, and transformation belong primarily to the erotic progression and entanglement layer. Events and named storylines connect the life tracks to that central erotic loop.

## Candidate core-path goals

Each life-direction system should have a visible achievement that changes the protagonist’s circumstances. These are baseline candidates for refinement rather than final objectives.

| Core path | Candidate goal | Meaning of achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **School and education** | Earn a scholarship or grant. | The protagonist proves they have an academic future. The award might fund further study, provide accommodation, or create an eventual opportunity outside the town. |
| **Work and economic role** | Accumulate the $10,000 independence fund. | Work becomes the direct material route towards a chosen future. Different jobs can contribute at different rates while creating their own risks and dependencies. |
| **Reputation and social position** | Become a recognised figure in the town. | The protagonist develops a public identity strong enough that people know them before meeting them. Its form might be respectable, notorious, sexually famous, successful, feared, or beloved. |
| **Relationships and dependence** | Form a committed future with another character. | The protagonist might leave with Robin, establish a household, accept protection, join a group, or enter a binding dependency. The achievement is no longer facing the future alone. |
| **Pregnancy and family** | Secure a future for the child before birth. | The protagonist works towards safe housing, money, support, recognition of parentage, adoption, or another deliberate outcome. Pregnancy becomes a life project rather than only a bodily consequence. |
| **Housing and independence** | Obtain a permanent home outside Bailey’s control. | The protagonist gains somewhere that is genuinely theirs. It could later become a safe house, household, business, relationship space, or base for another route. |

## Provisional routes into each core goal

Each core goal should remain recognisable while supporting several routes with different levels of visibility. Beginner routes should emerge naturally from ordinary play. More obscure routes can require unusual story progression, sexual specialisation, coercive bargains, or knowledge of hidden locations.

The routes below are proposed extensions grafted onto confirmed existing content. The listed storylines, locations, jobs, and state systems already exist, but their connection to the new core achievements would need to be developed.

### School and education: earn a grant

- **Academic route, obvious:** achieve consistently strong exam results across the normal subjects.
- **Science route, specialised:** win the existing science fair and build on the recognition already given by students afterwards.
- **Performance route, social:** succeed in the English play, gain social fame, and attract sponsorship through the existing Avery encounter.
- **Leighton route, corrupt:** use the existing blackmail and photoshoot storyline to force Leighton to approve, falsify, or privately fund the grant.

Existing anchors include `game/overworld-town/loc-school/exam.twee`, `science-project.twee`, `english-play.twee`, and `fame.twee`.

### Work and economic role: build the $10,000 fund

- **Ordinary employment, obvious:** steadily earn through office, domestic, service, or similar conventional work.
- **Farm route, involved:** work with Alex, restore the farm, and earn through its expanding activities.
- **Model route, specialised:** progress through photography jobs and convert modelling fame into increasingly valuable work.
- **Briar route, dangerous:** use brothel work, shows, the vending-machine business, and lucrative escort clients to earn the fund rapidly at the cost of deeper entanglement.

Existing anchors include `game/overworld-town/loc-office/jobstart.twee`, `game/overworld-plains/loc-farm/work.twee`, `game/overworld-town/loc-photography/jobs.twee`, and `game/overworld-town/loc-brothel/main.twee`.

### Reputation and social position: become recognised

- **Good reputation, obvious:** help people around town and build on River’s soup-kitchen work until the protagonist becomes known as dependable.
- **Social reputation, public:** use the English play, dance work, parties, and performance scenes to become a local personality.
- **Sexual reputation, notorious:** progress through the brothel, public shows, school photoshoots, and exhibitionism until the protagonist becomes a recognised sexual figure.
- **Fighting reputation, obscure:** repeatedly intervene in street violence, fight bullies, resist public threats, and accumulate scrap fame until the protagonist becomes someone aggressors recognise and fear.

Existing anchors include `game/overworld-town/loc-temple/soup-kitchen.twee`, `game/overworld-town/loc-school/english-play.twee`, `game/overworld-town/loc-brothel/main.twee`, and the street events under `game/overworld-town/loc-street/`.

### Relationships and dependence: form a committed future

- **Robin route, obvious:** develop the existing friendship or romance into an explicit agreement about a shared future.
- **Sydney route, formal:** continue the romance and temple trials into the already foreshadowed sanctioned union.
- **Alex route, domestic:** restore the farm together, share the cottage, and turn the existing relationship and family content into a permanent partnership.
- **Eden route, psychologically obscure:** allow captivity, trust, routine, and voluntary return to evolve into the protagonist choosing the cabin and Eden as their future.

Existing anchors include `game/overworld-town/special-robin/`, `game/overworld-town/special-sydney/`, `game/overworld-plains/loc-farm/`, and `game/overworld-forest/loc-cabin/`.

### Pregnancy and family: secure the child’s future

- **Hospital route, obvious:** use Harper, hospital births, and the children’s home to arrange a conventional supported outcome.
- **Alex route, domestic:** restore the farm, construct the existing cottage nursery, and retrieve or raise the children there.
- **Sydney route, religious:** use Sydney’s pregnancy dialogue and temple union storyline to have the pregnancy recognised and supported by the order.
- **Wolf route, hidden:** become accepted by the wolf pack, give birth through the existing wolf-cave content, and raise the resulting pups within the pack.

Existing anchors include `game/base-system/pregnancy/events.twee`, `game/base-system/pregnancy/children-events.twee`, `game/overworld-plains/loc-farm/upgrades.twee`, `game/overworld-town/special-sydney/temple.twee`, and `game/overworld-forest/loc-wolfpack/pregnancyEvents.twee`.

### Housing and independence: obtain a permanent home

- **Alex route, recognisable:** earn the right to stay at the cottage through farm progression, then develop it into a genuine shared home.
- **Avery route, compromised luxury:** progress until Avery pays Bailey and moves the protagonist into the mansion. The housing goal is achieved, but independence may not be.
- **Eden route, coercive domesticity:** remain or repeatedly return until the cabin becomes an accepted home rather than solely a place of captivity.
- **Wolf route, deeply hidden:** gain enough pack trust, accept a place within the pack, and make the cave a permanent non-human home.

Existing anchors include `game/overworld-plains/loc-farm/main.twee`, `game/overworld-town/special-avery/mansion intro.twee`, `game/overworld-forest/loc-cabin/`, and `game/overworld-forest/loc-wolfpack/`.

The shared design pattern is that the goal remains fixed while the route changes its meaning. A home might be earned, shared, provided by a patron, accepted as part of a coercive relationship, or found through membership in a non-human community. The route should determine the consequences, obligations, and future content attached to the achievement.

## 1. The visible starting goal

The opening should give the player one simple, concrete objective:

> Save enough money to leave town and start an independent life elsewhere.

A target such as $10,000 gives the player a visible horizon without requiring the whole game to become a conventional quest. It should be framed as an exit fund, not necessarily the only way to leave.

Possible interpretations:

- escape the orphanage and Bailey’s control;
- buy travel, documents, housing, and initial support elsewhere;
- leave with enough money to avoid immediately becoming dependent on another person;
- save for a specific destination or opportunity.

The amount can remain deliberately abstract during early design. It could be a literal fixed target, a variable cost, or one component of a broader independence requirement.

## 2. Early and mid-sized goals

The large goal should be broken into intermediate projects that connect existing systems.

Possible early goals:

- pay Bailey’s immediate debt;
- obtain reliable clothing, food, medicine, and transport;
- keep attending school while building income;
- find safer work or a more profitable sexual economy;
- acquire identification, contacts, or documents needed to leave;
- establish a private place to sleep or store belongings;
- identify trustworthy people who might help with departure.

Possible mid-sized goals:

- build a support network;
- become skilled enough to earn money without constant exploitation;
- protect Robin or arrange a shared escape;
- gain enough social reputation to access respectable opportunities;
- become known within a particular sexual or social subculture;
- secure sponsorship, employment, or protection from a powerful NPC;
- decide whether to leave alone, leave with someone, or remain and build a life locally.

These goals should not all be presented as formal quests. Some can be explicit, while others emerge through conversations, repeated encounters, rumours, and changes in NPC behaviour.

## 3. Multiple meanings of “escape”

Leaving town should not be the only possible form of success.

Candidate long-term outcomes include:

- **Physical escape:** leave town with sufficient resources.
- **Shared escape:** leave with Robin or another trusted character.
- **Protected escape:** secure sponsorship, employment, or legal protection from someone powerful.
- **Local independence:** remain in town but escape Bailey’s control and become self-sufficient.
- **Sexual integration:** become established within the town’s sexual economy and choose not to leave.
- **Relational belonging:** decide that a relationship or community is worth more than departure.
- **Psychological reversal:** no longer want to leave because the protagonist’s desires, attachments, or identity have changed.
- **Failed or compromised escape:** technically leave, but arrive elsewhere dependent, damaged, indebted, or attached to the same structures.

These should be treated as different stories rather than a good/bad ending ladder.

## 4. The counterforce: entanglement

The protagonist should not be able to solve the exit goal through unlimited income alone. The main counterforce should be increasing entanglement rather than a moral punishment for sexual behaviour.

Possible forms of entanglement:

- **Economic:** debt, unstable income, dependency on clients, employers, or protectors.
- **Social:** reputation that closes some routes while opening others.
- **Relational:** lovers, owners, clients, friends, or dependants who create reasons to stay.
- **Legal and practical:** missing documents, blackmail, pregnancy, obligations, or lack of safe transport.
- **Psychological:** trauma, stress, loss of control, conditioning, or reduced capacity to plan.
- **Identity-based:** the protagonist becomes known or recognised in a way that makes anonymity elsewhere difficult.
- **Desire-based:** the protagonist develops attachments, preferences, or appetites that conflict with the original escape plan.
- **Physical:** injury, transformation, pregnancy, exhaustion, or other states that make travel and independence harder.

These pressures should compete with the money goal rather than simply negate it. A player may become richer while becoming less able or less willing to leave.

## 5. Sexual routes as life trajectories

Existing sexual systems should be able to become paths through the larger goal structure.

A route should not unlock merely because a hidden stat crossed a threshold. It should emerge from repeated behaviour, recognition, relationships, locations, and consequences.

Candidate route families:

- prostitution and commercial dependency;
- exhibitionism and public reputation;
- submission, ownership, or protection;
- resistance, defiance, and self-protection;
- corruption or deliberate surrender to the town’s culture;
- relationship-centred escape;
- non-human or bestiality-related routes;
- wilderness or creature-associated belonging;
- performance, fame, or public sexual identity.

For example, a bestiality-related route could develop through several possible directions:

- repeated encounters create recognition from a creature, owner, handler, or local group;
- the protagonist discovers a hidden location or community associated with those encounters;
- the route offers safety, income, belonging, or an alternative escape method;
- public discovery creates reputation and social consequences;
- the protagonist becomes increasingly attached to that route, whether willingly or through accumulated dependency.

The same starting behaviour could therefore lead to different outcomes depending on whether it is pursued, endured, commercialised, hidden, or connected to a particular NPC.

The same route structure could be applied to exhibitionism, prostitution, submission, resistance, named-NPC relationships, or other recurring sexual patterns.

## 6. Continuity after sexual encounters

Every significant sexual encounter should have the potential to affect the protagonist’s larger project through one or more channels:

- money gained or lost;
- time and energy consumed;
- injury, trauma, or recovery needs;
- reputation and discoverability;
- NPC attachment or hostility;
- new opportunities or obligations;
- changes in desire, comfort, or self-concept;
- access to a new place, group, route, or resource;
- reasons to leave;
- reasons to stay.

This does not mean every scene needs a visible quest reward. The purpose is to make the protagonist’s life accumulate direction over time.

The player should be able to look back and understand that encounters changed the trajectory, even when the immediate result was simply survival, pleasure, humiliation, or escape.

## 7. Agency and uncertainty

The goal structure should support the existing erotic design rather than turning it into a conventional empowerment game.

The protagonist can pursue independence, but:

- money should not guarantee safety;
- reputation should have mixed effects;
- NPCs should retain independent motives;
- sexual choices should create leverage, not total control;
- route access should be discoverable but not completely transparent;
- failure should redirect the story rather than always end it;
- consensual, transactional, ambiguous, and coercive encounters should remain meaningfully distinct.

The desired player feeling is:

> “My character was trying to build a life, and this encounter changed what that life could become.”

Not:

> “I selected a scene from a menu and then returned to the real game.”

## 8. Exploratory refinement phases

### Phase A: establish the protagonist’s starting project

Decide how explicitly the opening states the escape or independence goal and whether the first target is a fixed amount, a practical requirement, or a symbolic milestone.

### Phase B: map existing systems to goal pressure

For each major system, identify whether it primarily provides:

- progress toward independence;
- a cost or setback;
- a new route;
- a source of attachment;
- a reason to stay;
- a way to reinterpret the protagonist’s future.

### Phase C: identify route families

Select several possible route families for experimentation without committing to final endings. Include at least one conventional escape route, one relationship route, and one route based on sexual or non-human integration.

### Phase D: prototype accumulated consequences

Test whether a small number of encounters can produce visibly different life trajectories through money, reputation, relationships, trauma, and desire without requiring a complete rewrite of the game.

### Phase E: refine endings and failure states

Decide whether outcomes should be discrete endings, stable ongoing states, or both. A “failed escape” may still be playable if it creates a new situation rather than simply displaying a conclusion.

## Success criteria

The design is working if:

- a new player understands what the protagonist is trying to accomplish;
- school, work, money, and relationships feel connected to that project;
- sex scenes remain the primary content rather than becoming secondary to questing;
- repeated sexual experiences produce recognisable life trajectories;
- different routes offer different kinds of success rather than simple moral rankings;
- sexual behaviour can create both opportunities and entanglements;
- the player can pursue departure, belonging, exploitation, protection, or surrender as meaningful alternatives;
- the protagonist feels like they have an ongoing life plan, even when the player deliberately derails it.

## Open questions for later refinement

- Is the $10,000 target literal, symbolic, or variable?
- Can the protagonist leave early with insufficient resources, creating a compromised outcome?
- Should Robin have a parallel goal, or primarily react to the protagonist’s choices?
- Which route families deserve early prototypes?
- How much should the protagonist’s changing desire influence whether they still want to leave?
- Should reputation affect the ability to leave, the destination available, or only the life left behind?
- Are routes primarily discovered through explicit dialogue, hidden state, or cumulative environmental feedback?
- Should the game eventually allow the protagonist to change the town, or only change their position within it?
